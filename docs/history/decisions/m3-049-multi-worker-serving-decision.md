# Multi-worker serving decision (M3-049)

> **Current.** Now described in [serving reference, "Workers"](../../reference/serving.md#workers) and [ARCHITECTURE, "Flare adapter"](../../ARCHITECTURE.md#flare-adapter).
> Changed in part since:
> - [M3-060](m3-060-tls-with-workers-decision.md): reversed the refusal of a TLS Server on workers.
> - [M3-068](m3-068-worker-connection-spread-decision.md): split what the workers guarantee: a connection opened while one worker is already running a handler is answered by another idle worker (a tested contract); one opened just before a worker starts a slow handler is not a contract.
> Its pointers into documents that were split or renamed since (`docs/DX.md`, old `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` headings) are mapped in [the list of records](../architecture-decisions.md#documents-older-records-cite).

Status: **decision and production** (M3-049; `src/muntin` unchanged). It revisits [M3-046](m3-046-head-of-line-blocking-decision.md), which kept one reactor thread because running `App.handle` on several threads could make a `State` value with unsynchronized interior mutability a data race, and Muntin cannot require at compile time that a value is safe to share. Measurements, mutations and the probes behind the facts below are in the pull request.

**Question.** Can `Server` serve on several threads, as Flare's `serve(handler, num_workers)` does, with the compiler, not the application's discipline, ruling out a value reached from two threads? Settled by: what Mojo 1.1.0 can check (read and probed with the installed compiler); what Flare v0.12.0's multi-worker path offers (read in code), and whether M3-045's stop (new connections refused at once, open ones served for the window) can hold with it; and wire tests in child processes, M3-046's held-pipe form, showing what improves and what does not, with scratch mutations that turn them red.

**Choice: one `App` per worker, built on that worker.** `server.serve[make](workers=n)` takes `make: def() thin raises -> App` and `workers` (1 to 256, keyword-only), and each worker thread calls `make()` once, then serves its own `App` with Flare's reactor loop. Nothing is shared, so the M3-001 account (an `App` and its `State` values are used by one thread at a time) holds per worker unchanged, and the compiler checks the boundary: a `thin` function captures nothing (a compile-time closure that captures is `capturing`, refused by the type; a run-time closure is not a compile-time value), and Mojo 1.1.0 has no module-level `var`. What Muntin does not check, and says so: `make` reaching shared memory through an unsafe pointer from an address, `_get_global` or a C call. Flare's own multi-worker `serve` is not used: its handler must be `Copyable` and is copied on the starting thread with an immutable `serve(self)`, so an `App` could not be built on its worker; and its stop does not keep M3-045's contract (`HttpServer.drain` does not reach the workers' listeners; `Scheduler.drain` stops every reactor at once, with no window). So the adapter starts the workers itself (one pthread each, `run_unified_reactor_loop_shared` on a close-on-exec copy of the bound listener, so a program the application starts with `exec` cannot keep the port (a child it forks without `exec` inherits every descriptor, the `Server`'s listener included, as with `serve(app)`, and keeps the port while it lives); a stop byte per worker) and its watcher does Flare's single-reactor `drain` for all of them: park every copy on a pipe that never becomes readable (`dup2`, so no number is reused under a reactor) and close the `Server`'s listener, wait the window, set every stop byte; a worker ending during the window does not cut it short. A copy that cannot be parked is kept open until the joins, and the stop becomes a hard one that `serve` raises for. A worker ending on its own before a stop signal was delivered (as the serving thread sees it at its next look, every `_WATCH_POLL_MS`, not when the worker ended, which nothing records; and read from the dispositions, not from the watcher's last look) stops the others at once, and `serve` raises what ended it, recorded then (a `make` or reactor raise, or a reactor that returned: Flare's loop returns on a failed poll); a `make`, a handler or an `App` destructor that does not return cannot be interrupted, so if a worker has not ended (its `App` destroyed) `_REACTOR_STOP_MS` later the process ends with status 1, as the watcher ends it past its own bound. A TLS `Server` is refused: the workers' loop is not given its context, and no test covers a handshake on a worker. `serve(app)`, `Server.bind`, `_BorrowedHandler`'s non-`Copyable` guard and every existing test are unchanged. What improves (tested, HTTP/1.1 and h2c, 2 workers, 1 s hold): a connection opened while one worker's handler runs is answered by another, before the held handler returns. What does not: a connection stays on the worker that accepted it, so the held worker's open connections, and every other stream of one h2c connection, still wait, and are still dropped after a hold longer than the idle timeout. Each `State` is per worker; one value seen by every worker is not available.

**Rejected:**
- A marker trait for shareable state (`State[S: ThreadSafe]`) and one `App` shared by every worker: Mojo 1.1.0 has no compiler-checked notion of thread-safe sharing (no `Send`/`Sync`; nothing in the standard library), so conformance would be the application's claim, which an `ArcPointer[Int]` written without synchronization could make; checking it per route would also need the `App` to carry its state types. That is the unchecked rule M3-046 rejected.
- Flare's `HttpServer.serve(handler, num_workers)` with a `Copyable` handler: no place to build an `App` on its worker (above), and M3-045's stop would break.
- Flare's `Scheduler` with a Muntin `Frontend`: it builds per worker (a probe served with it), but its `drain` has no window and the listeners it binds are its own, so the stop would still need the adapter's.
- Keep M3-046's deferral: the user-visible cost (one slow handler stops every new connection of the process) has a checked remedy now.

**Revisit when:**
- an application needs one `State` value shared by every worker, or Mojo gains a compiler-checked notion of thread-safe sharing: a shared-state decision of its own (a bound on `S`, or Muntin-owned synchronized types);
- TLS with workers is wanted: pass the `Server`'s context to `run_unified_reactor_loop_shared` (it takes one) and test a handshake on every worker;
- the Flare pin moves: `adapters/flare/flare_internals.mojo` holds every Flare internal the adapter uses (`run_unified_reactor_loop_shared` and its stop byte, `HttpServer`'s private listener and TLS fields, Flare's thread and pipe) with what each is relied on for; check those against the new release, re-run the workers tests, and check whether Flare's multi-worker `drain` now keeps a window and closes every listener, which would let the adapter use Flare's own path;
- the workers tests' observations change (a new connection not answered during a hold, a stream of the held h2c connection answered during it, a request answered off the thread that built its `App`, a stop that accepts or ends early): `docs/DX.md` section 1 and this record;
- `compat/flare/serve/serve_workers_capturing_make.mojo` or `serve_workers_runtime_closure.mojo` builds: `make` can capture, and the boundary is gone.

**Next production slice (M3-049): `Server.serve[make](workers=n)`.** Implemented with this record: the adapter's worker table, `_serve_worker[make]`, `_drain_workers` and the serving thread's join (`docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`, "Backend seam"); `docs/DX.md` section 1, "Workers"; tests in `adapters/flare/test_server.mojo`, "Workers (M3-049)": a new HTTP/1.1 and a new h2c connection answered during a hold, a stream of the held h2c connection not (with a long and with the default idle timeout), one `make` per worker and every answer on its `App`'s thread, the graceful stop, a `make` raising during the window, the range, a raising `make`, the buffer-ring refusal and the `docs/DX.md` example; after review, a raising `make` beside a worker stuck in `make`, in a handler or in its `App`'s destructor, a stop delivered before the watcher saw it (with a worker left, and as the last one ends), a stop while workers start, a reactor returning before a stop, a process started by a handler outliving `serve`, a copy that cannot be parked (alone, and between two that are), a TLS `Server`, a stop while connections keep arriving and 256 workers; and the two must-not-build fixtures above.
