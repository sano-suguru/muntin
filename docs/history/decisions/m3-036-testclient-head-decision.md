# TestClient HEAD decision (M3-036)

> **Current.** Now described in [testing reference, "`TestClient`"](../../reference/testing.md#testclient), [routing reference, "`HEAD`"](../../reference/routing.md#head) and [ARCHITECTURE, "Backend seam"](../../ARCHITECTURE.md#backend-seam).
> Its pointers into documents that were split or renamed since (`docs/DX.md`, old `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` headings) are mapped in [the list of records](../architecture-decisions.md#documents-older-records-cite).

Status: **decision** (M3-036, PR #74; `src/muntin`, `adapters/` and `compat/` are unchanged in it). It decides whether `TestClient` gains a `head` method and what that method's response carries. The question and what settles it were committed before any measurement (`docs/DEVELOPMENT.md` section 2).

**Why now.** M3-026 kept `TestClient` without `head` (its candidate E) and recorded the revisit condition "tests need `HEAD` through `TestClient`: add `TestClient.head(target, *, headers=)` (candidate E, M3-010's pattern)". It fired in the M3-035 review: `tests/test_middleware.mojo` sends every request of its set through `TestClient` except `HEAD`, which it sends through `App.handle`, and DX section 7 says so. M3-026 decided where the content of a `HEAD` response is dropped (the network backend) but not what an in-memory `head` method returns, and the two readings disagree: `TestClient` builds a `Request` and calls `App.handle`, nothing else (M3-010's invariant), which keeps the `GET` body; while its module calls it the "in-memory backend", and a network backend sends no content for `HEAD` (M3-026's invariant).

**Question.** What does `TestClient.head(target, *, headers=)` return? Specifically, whether its response keeps the body `App.handle(Request("HEAD", target, "", headers^))` returns (the `GET`'s, M3-027), or removes it, as a network backend keeps it off the wire; if it removes it, whether it also declares a `Content-Length` and for which statuses; whether `TestClient` is, for this purpose, a backend with M3-026's framing duty or an in-memory caller of `App.handle`; and which statements of DX, ARCHITECTURE and the earlier records (M3-010's invariant, M3-026's invariants and its "in-memory backend" sentence) change.

**What settles it:**
- the existing `TestClient` (`src/muntin/testing.mojo`) traced: how each method builds its `Request` (method, target split by `Request`, body, `headers=` moved in), its borrow of the `App`, and who owns the returned `Response`; and whether a `head` method needs anything beyond the pattern the five methods share;
- the existing contracts, read from their records and tests: M3-010's invariant that the client's answer equals `App.handle(Request(...))` for the same arguments; M3-026's and M3-027's rule that `App.handle` answers `HEAD` through `GET` routes with the body kept, the network backend drops it and declares the length, and the in-memory response has the body; M3-005's rule that `Content-Length` is the backend's; M3-034's rule that middleware sees `HEAD` as `HEAD`;
- per candidate, a spike built with Mojo 1.1.0 in the default environment (a client over the unchanged production `App`, `Request` and `Response` under `tests/`, production untouched): its answer, compared with `App.handle(Request("HEAD", ...))` and with `App.handle(Request("GET", ...))`, for a typed `get` route, a raw `get` route (which sees `req.method == "HEAD"`), a stateful route, route values and a query, `headers=` reaching a `Headers` slot, 404, 405 with `Allow`, a route-value 400, a handler's 500, raw routes answering 204, 205 and 304 with a body, and middleware that observes `HEAD` as `HEAD`, adds a field, short-circuits and replaces the response;
- for a candidate that removes the body: what it must reproduce of the network rule to be faithful to it (the `Content-Length` it declares, the statuses that carry none), compared with what the Flare adapter sends, taken from existing evidence (`compat/flare/head/head_probe.mojo`, `adapters/flare/test_muntin_flare.mojo`, `adapters/flare/test_localhost_roundtrip.mojo`, and the M3-026 record's measured premises) without a new network probe unless that evidence leaves the question open;
- each candidate's cost: code in `testing.mojo`, copies of `Request` or `Response`, whether a framing rule (a status set, a header field) enters `src/muntin`, and what a test author could misread.

**Boundaries that do not change silently:** `HEAD` routing (a `get` route answers it, M3-026), `App.handle` never removing a body, the Flare adapter's `HEAD` rule and the HTTP framing it sends, middleware's contract (M3-034), the five existing `TestClient` methods and their `headers=` spelling (M3-010), the registration API, `Request`, `Response` and `Headers`.

**Candidates to measure:**
- A: `head` returns `App.handle(Request("HEAD", target, "", headers^))` unchanged, body included;
- B: `head` removes the body of that response, as a network backend keeps it off the wire (with or without a declared `Content-Length`, as the measurement shows a faithful form needs);
- baseline: no `head` method (main today).

**The client today.** `TestClient[origin]` holds a `Pointer[App, origin]` to a borrowed `App` (`TestClient(app)` copies nothing; `App.handle` takes `self` read-only). Each of its five methods is one line: it builds `Request(method, target, body, headers^)` (`get` and `delete` with `""` as the body), so `Request`'s initializer splits the target at its first `?`, moves the keyword-only, defaulted `headers` in, and the method returns `App.handle`'s `Response` to the caller by value. No method adds, removes or reads a field, the body or the route (M3-010's invariant). A `head` method in that pattern is `get` with `"HEAD"` for `"GET"`: no new type, abstraction, field or helper.

**The existing contracts.** `App.handle` answers `HEAD` through `GET` routes with the `GET`'s body and never removes a body (M3-026, M3-027); middleware sees `HEAD` as `HEAD` and the backend drops the content of whatever comes out (M3-034, M3-035); `Content-Length` and the connection fields are the backend's, dropped by the adapter from every answer (M3-005). M3-026 assigns the framing duty to network backends: its M2 reopen says "keeping a `HEAD` response's content off the wire and its length is each network backend's obligation", it says "The in-memory backend has no wire: `App.handle(Request("HEAD", ...))` shows the response with its body", and `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` and `docs/SPEC.md` say "each network backend". Its invariant "A backend sends no content for `HEAD`" says "a backend" without "network"; this record reads it as the network backend its own paragraphs name, and does not reword it. M0 calls `TestClient` an in-memory backend because it builds a Muntin `Request`, calls `App.handle` and hands back the `Response`; this record reads that as M3-026 ("The in-memory backend has no wire") and `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` ("in-memory backend / TestClient") do: a caller of the backend seam with no wire, whose answer is `App.handle`'s, not a network backend with M3-026's framing duty.

**Measured premises.** Mojo 1.1.0 (`8189361e`), the default environment, Linux. `tests/test_spike_testclient_head.mojo` (13 tests, `--Werror`, all pass) puts three candidate methods on a `SpikeClient` over the unchanged production `App`, `Request`, `Response` and `TestClient`: A (`head_keep`), B1 (`head_drop`: A's response with the body set to `""`) and B2 (`head_framed`: B1 plus a `Content-Length` of the removed body's byte length, none for 1xx, 204, 205 and 304, the adapter's `_without_content` rule copied). The app: typed `get` routes (`/hello`, `/users/{id}`, `/fields/{id}?{field}`, `/h/{id}` with a `Headers` slot), a stateful one (`/count/{id}`), a raising one (`/fail`), raw `get` routes (`/report` with a field of its own, which logs each method it receives; `/lazy`, which breaks DX's raw rule by answering `HEAD` with an empty body; `/cl`, which sets its own `Content-Length: 99`; `/nc`, `/rc`, `/nm` answering 204, 205 and 304 with a body) and a raw `post` route (`/only-post`). Observations:
- A equals `App.handle(Request("HEAD", target))` (status, body, every field in order) on the 13 targets, which cover a typed route, a route value, a query value, a stateful route, a raw route, the 400 for a bad route value and for a missing query value, the handler's 500, the 405 with `Allow: POST`, the 404 and the raw 204, 205 and 304; and it equals `TestClient.get` on each of them (every route there answers `HEAD` as `GET`; the 405's `Allow` is the same for both methods). Bodies: `user 7`, `Bad Request`, `Internal Server Error`, `Not Found`, and `abc` on the 304.
- A sends `HEAD`: the raw route logs `HEAD;` then `GET;` for `head_keep` then `TestClient.get` (a stateful route also counts one call, which does not depend on the method). `headers=` reaches the `Headers` slot (`1 X-A=b`), equal to `App.handle` with the same fields.
- A with middleware (`seen` adds `X-Seen` with the method it received, `gate` answers 403 for `/count/...` without running the rest, `replace_missing` replaces a 404 with its own): equal to `App.handle` on every target; `HEAD /hello` carries `X-Seen: HEAD`; `HEAD /count/1` is the 403 with the handler's count at 0; `HEAD /missing` is `custom missing`.
- A shows a raw handler breaking the `HEAD` rule: `head_keep("/lazy").body` is `""` where `get("/lazy").body` is `lazy body`, the difference that makes a network backend declare a wrong length (DX section 4). B1 hides it: `head_drop("/lazy")` equals `head_drop("/hello")` (200, `""`, no field). B1 also hides which 404 came back: Muntin's and a middleware's replacement are equal under B1 and differ under A.
- B1 differs from `App.handle` in the body only, on every target. It declares no length, so it carries less than A on every target, and less than the adapter's answer on every status that carries content; on 204, 205 and 304 it equals, by code reading, the adapter's converted answer (no body, no length, as `adapters/flare/test_muntin_flare.mojo`'s `test_head_declares_no_length_for_statuses_without_content` asserts for the adapter's own routes; the spike's `/nc`, `/rc` and `/nm` set no field the adapter drops).
- B2 declares `Content-Length: 6` (`/users/7`), `11` (`/users/abc`), `Allow: POST; Content-Length: 18` (`/only-post`), `X-Report: 1; Content-Length: 6` (`/report`), `0` (`/lazy`), and no field for 204, 205 and 304. `App.handle` returns no `Content-Length` on any of the 13 targets, so B2's field is one only the client adds. On `/cl` B2 answers `Content-Length: 99; Content-Length: 3`: the adapter drops a handler's `Content-Length` before its `HEAD` rule (M3-005; `adapters/flare/test_muntin_flare.mojo`, `test_head_sends_get_fields_and_length_without_body`, `/cl` gives `X-Request-Id: 7`, `Content-Length: 3`), and B2, copying the rule alone, keeps both. A answers `/cl` with the handler's field, as `TestClient.get` does for `GET`.
- On each of the 13 targets where B2 declares a length, it equals the byte length of A's body: what B2 adds is already in A (on `/cl`, outside the 13, B2's first `Content-Length` is the handler's 99). By code reading, B2 applies the adapter's `_without_content` rule to `App.handle`'s answer, so it equals the adapter's converted answer wherever `App.handle` returns no field the adapter drops or refuses (all 13 targets); it differs where one is returned (`/cl`).
- B1 and B2 are fixed functions of A's response (the spike's `head_drop` and `head_framed` call the same `App.handle` and only then change the response), so what A shows about the request the app receives (the method, `headers=`, what middleware sees) holds for them by construction; it was measured on A only. A's response equalities do not carry over.
- A's equality with `App.handle` holds by construction: `head_keep` is the one-line body the slice ships. What the spike shows for A is that it needs nothing beyond that line, that `HEAD` reaches the application as `HEAD`, and how B1 and B2 differ from it; it is not independent evidence that A is right, which rests on the existing contracts ("Why A").
- Mutations (planted in scratch copies of the spike, not retained; 4), each red: A sending `GET` (4 tests: the raw route's method log, `X-Seen`, and both `/lazy` tests, `test_a_shows_a_raw_handler_breaking_the_head_rule` and `test_b1_hides_a_raw_handler_breaking_the_head_rule`; the equality with `App.handle(Request("HEAD", ...))` alone does not catch it, because each of the 13 targets answers `HEAD` as `GET`); A dropping `headers` (1); B1 keeping the body (3); B2 declaring a length for 304 (1).
- The wire, from existing evidence, with no new network probe: through the Flare adapter, `HEAD` gets no content and `Content-Length` equal to the `GET` body's byte length over HTTP/1.1 and h2c; for 205 and 304 the adapter declares none, and Flare then frames `Content-Length: 0` over HTTP/1.1 and sends no `content-length` over h2c (`compat/flare/head/head_probe.mojo`, observations 1b and 1c; M3-026's measured premises); Flare adds a `Date` field (`compat/flare/head/head_probe.mojo`'s HTTP/1.1 observations) and h2c lowercases names; the adapter drops a handler's `Content-Length` and the connection fields and answers 500 for an outgoing field it cannot represent (`adapters/flare/test_muntin_flare.mojo`). So a `HEAD` answer on the wire is not one value: it depends on the protocol, on Flare's own framing and on the adapter's whole outbound policy, of which the `HEAD` rule is one step.

**Selected: A. `TestClient.head(target, *, headers=)` builds `Request("HEAD", target, "", headers^)`, calls `App.handle`, and returns its `Response` unchanged, body and fields as `App.handle` returned them.** Through a `get` route that is the `GET`'s body when the handler and every middleware treat `HEAD` as `GET` (every typed route does); a raw handler or middleware that answers `HEAD` differently gets its own answer back (`/lazy` above). `TestClient` stays an in-memory caller of `App.handle` with no wire: it removes no body, declares no `Content-Length` and applies no status rule. M3-026's framing duty stays the network backend's.

```mojo
var client = TestClient(app)
var head = client.head("/users/7")            # = app.handle(Request("HEAD", "/users/7"))
# head.status == 200, head.text() == "user 7": the GET's answer; a network backend sends no content
var h = Headers()
h.add("X-Request-Id", "42")
_ = client.head("/users/7", headers=h^)       # fields as on every TestClient method
```

- **Contract.** `def head(self, target: String, *, var headers: Headers = Headers()) -> Response`. `target` is a path with an optional query, split by `Request`; `headers` keyword-only, defaulted to none, moved in (M3-010's pattern, which its revisit condition "`TestClient` gains another request method" asks the new method to follow). The request body is empty, as for `get` and `delete`; a test that needs a `HEAD` body builds the `Request` and calls `App.handle`.
- **Method.** The handler and every middleware see `HEAD`: a typed `get` handler gets the `GET`'s arguments, a raw `get` handler `req.method == "HEAD"`, middleware `request.method == "HEAD"`.
- **Status, body, fields.** Exactly `App.handle`'s: the `get` route's answer to `HEAD`, 400 for a bad route value, 404, 405 with Muntin's `Allow`, the fixed 500, or whatever middleware returned, each with its body. No field is added or removed, so a `Content-Length` appears only when a handler or middleware set one (the adapter would drop it).
- **Errors.** None of its own: `head` does not raise, and a raise inside the application is `App.handle`'s 500.
- **Why A.** It is the answer the existing contracts already define for this request (M3-010: the client's answer equals `App.handle`'s; M3-026: the in-memory response keeps the body), so no record changes and nothing in `src/muntin` learns framing. It is the one candidate with which a test can check the input of the network rule: the body whose length the backend declares (a raw handler or middleware that shortens it shows up). B1 removes exactly that information and makes different answers look equal; B2 reconstructs one step of one adapter's outbound policy: it equals the adapter's converted answer on the spike's 13 targets (by code reading) but not where a handler sets a backend-owned field (`/cl`, measured), and neither B1 nor B2 equals Flare's bytes (the `Date` field; HTTP/1.1's `Content-Length: 0` on 205 and 304). Neither equals `App.handle`'s answer, which M3-010's invariant makes the client's. What a network backend sends for `HEAD` is already tested where it is produced: the adapter's contract tests, the loopback suite and the `HEAD` probe.

**Candidates.**

| Candidate | Verdict |
|---|---|
| A: return `App.handle`'s answer, body kept | **chosen** |
| B1: remove the body | rejected: measured, it hides a raw handler or middleware that answers `HEAD` with a different body (`/lazy`, the replaced 404), declares no length, so it never matches `App.handle` when the body is non-empty, nor the adapter's answer for a status that carries content, and breaks M3-010's invariant that the client's answer equals `App.handle`'s. Nothing a test can assert under B1 is unavailable under A (`head.body` is simply not asserted, or asserted equal to the `GET`'s) |
| B2: remove the body and declare `Content-Length` by M3-026's rule | rejected: it breaks M3-010's invariant, and a framing rule (a status set, a backend-owned field) enters `src/muntin`, a second copy of `_without_content` to keep in step; measured, the spike's B2 returns two `Content-Length` fields where the adapter sends one, because the adapter's other outbound rules (M3-005's dropped fields, the 500 for an unrepresentable field) are not copied; a B2 that dropped the handler's field first would avoid that only by copying one more of those rules, so the objection is the copied policy, not the duplicate. Flare's own framing of 205 and 304 over HTTP/1.1 differs again; `Headers.add` raises, so `head` would have to swallow an error or raise, unlike the five methods. What it adds equals A's body length, measured |
| baseline: no `head` (main) | rejected: M3-026's condition fired; a test sending `HEAD` leaves the client for `App.handle` (`tests/test_middleware.mojo`), though nothing about `HEAD` needs that |

**M2 contract: not reopened.** The accepted set grows by one client method; no existing call, answer, overload or diagnostic changes; `App.handle`, the adapter and the wire are unchanged.

**Invariants:**
- `TestClient.head(target, headers=h)` equals `App.handle(Request("HEAD", target, "", h))`: status, body and every field in order. M3-010's invariant covers `head` as it covers the other five methods.
- `TestClient` removes no body and adds no field for any method; the content and length of a `HEAD` response are each network backend's (M3-026).

**Cost.** A test author who expects a wire-like `HEAD` (`curl -I`) sees a body; the docs say the in-memory answer keeps it, as `App.handle`'s does today. A test cannot see through `TestClient` what a network backend sends for `HEAD`; that stays the adapter's tests'. The method costs what `get` costs: one `Request`, `App.handle`'s `Response` returned by move, no copy of either beyond what `get` makes (read from the code; not timed).

**Revisit when:**
- tests need to assert, without a socket, what a network backend sends for `HEAD`: that belongs to the backend (an adapter-side helper over `_serve_app`), not `TestClient`; revisit B only if a second backend makes one in-memory wire answer well-defined;
- `App.handle` starts removing bodies, or streaming responses are decided (M3-026's revisit conditions): `head` follows `App.handle` unchanged, but the docs saying the in-memory answer keeps the body change;
- `TestClient` gains a generic `request(method, ...)` (M3-020, candidate E): revisit whether `head` stays one of the per-method set;
- `Request`'s initializer changes its ownership of headers (M3-010's condition): `head` follows the other methods.
