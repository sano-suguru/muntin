# Backend coupling decision (M3-070)

> **Current.** Now described in [ARCHITECTURE, "Flare policy"](../../ARCHITECTURE.md#flare-policy), [ARCHITECTURE, "Backend seam"](../../ARCHITECTURE.md#backend-seam) and [testing reference, "The backend seam"](../../reference/testing.md#the-backend-seam).
> Its pointers into documents that were split or renamed since (`docs/DX.md`, old `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` headings) are mapped in [the list of records](../architecture-decisions.md#documents-older-records-cite).

Status: **decision, standing constraint** (M3-070, pull request #128; `src/muntin` and the adapter's code unchanged; `scripts/check_flare_reliance.sh` added). The line counts, the count of `docs/DX.md` section 1's statements and the map from each relied-on behavior to the test that observes it are in the pull request.

**Question and what settles it.** An outside review of pull request #120 judged that the Flare adapter no longer adapts Flare but builds one server together with Flare's internals: `adapters/flare/flare_internals.mojo` reaches Flare's private reactor loop and its stop byte, `HttpServer`'s listener and TLS context address, and Flare's thread and pipe, and the adapter runs its own stop watcher, worker table and listener parking around them; an import boundary does not make that backend replaceable, and the coupling should weigh more as debt. Pull request #111 listed each Flare name with its public alternative and #117 proposes replacing one, but neither says what replacing the backend costs or how a new reliance is weighed. What does "replaceable backend" hold for today, and what must an item that adds coupling show? Settled by reading what a second backend would keep and rewrite, how much of `docs/DX.md` section 1, `Server`'s contract, states Flare's behavior, and which relied-on behaviors a test observes.

**Choice: accept the coupling as the Flare adapter's, draw the boundary in three layers, and make every new reliance a weighed, tested and counted cost.** `App.handle` and everything behind it is backend-independent, and checked to be (A2, A3). What a network backend owes Muntin's semantics (one `App` never called from two threads at once, the raw target, unchanged body bytes, `HEAD`) is Muntin's contract and carries over. Serving does not: `Server`, its settings, its threads (which keep each `App` on one, so a `State` value's interior mutability needs no lock; a backend that calls an `App` from several threads in turn must order the calls, and its `State` values must be safe on another thread), the workers (which decide how many `App`s exist and so what a `State` value counts) and the stop are the Flare adapter's. With workers, the stop watcher, worker table and listener parking (with `Server` itself, about 1,100 of `muntin_flare.mojo`'s 1,879 lines) drive Flare's per-worker loop through its stop byte and rely on how that loop registers, polls and leaves, and about a third of the sentences of `docs/DX.md` section 1 describe what Flare v0.12.0 does. So another backend keeps an application's source and its `TestClient` tests, not what it serves or how. With one network backend this is designed, not demonstrated. That is accepted: each alternative below either leaves new reliance unweighed, moves names without removing the reliance, gives up shipped behavior or crosses a product boundary, and A7 rules out a serving abstraction over one backend. What is not accepted is coupling that grows unweighed or unseen. A new reliance on a non-public Flare name, or on a behavior of Flare's, OpenSSL's or the operating system's that their documentation does not state (conversion included), is named where the code relies on it; has a test that fails when it stops holding, which says whether it observes the condition or only an outcome and what it misses, or else a statement of what a break does and what contains it, with the remaining risk named; and is counted in a record against Flare's public API and a proposal to Flare's maintainers, with the adapter's size before and after. The adapter's modules (its tests aside) past 2,500 lines together (2,111 now) wait for a decision of their own. `scripts/check_flare_reliance.sh` makes a new non-public name, or that size, fail `check.sh` until it is listed or decided. It is a cost of its own: a statement reader in shell and awk, about 310 lines with 230 of tests, that must follow Mojo's syntax and fails closed on what it does not read; the rule, its scope and the swap case are owned by `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`, "Flare policy", and the two existing reliances with no such test by its "Flare adapter" section. A "Relied on" line is a claim, not a check: `run_worker_loop`'s said that the loop registers the listener with `EPOLLEXCLUSIVE` on Linux, which Linux refuses for the mask Flare v0.12.0 asks for (Flare then registers without it), and no test noticed (found by an `strace` in pull request #120's investigation; this record's pull request corrects the line).

**Rejected.**
- Leaving it to #111's inventory: it localizes a change of name or signature, not of behavior, and nothing makes a new reliance a cost; the first revision of M3-068 (pull request #120) chose to rely on a kernel's wake-up order, and nothing required it to weigh that.
- Replacing the Flare internals with Muntin's own (`pthread_create`, `pipe`, a `std.atomic` stop store): the names move, the reliance on Flare's loop stays, and a copied protocol keeps compiling after Flare changes it (the reason #117 keeps Flare's own stop store).
- Flare's public multi-worker path (`HttpServer.serve(handler, num_workers)`, `Scheduler`, `HttpFrontend.run_worker`): [M3-049](m3-049-multi-worker-serving-decision.md)'s and #111's reasons (a `Copyable` handler, a stop with no window, a lost start error).
- A backend-neutral serving contract or trait now: one network backend (A7), so what a second would share is unknown.
- Dropping the stop watcher or the workers to keep the adapter thin: undoes [M3-045](m3-045-graceful-shutdown-decision.md) and M3-049.
- Vendoring or forking Flare's reactor: Muntin would own a reactor, which `AGENTS.md` and `docs/SPEC.md`'s product boundaries exclude.

**Revisit when:**
- a second network backend is started: decide what of `docs/reference/serving.md` both serve, and whether `Server` becomes a shared contract;
- Flare publishes a per-worker loop that borrows a listener and keeps its start error, or a multi-worker `drain` with a window: the adapter can drop internals (M3-049's revisit);
- the Flare pin moves: check every "Relied on" statement against the release and against the tests that observe it;
- the rule stops an item the owner wants to take anyway, or the adapter nears its 2,500 lines: amend this record rather than skip the rule.
- the script's reader needs changing for new Mojo syntax, or refuses code the adapter needs: weigh a compiler-based check, or a narrower one (imports only), against extending it.

**Next production slice:** none (a standing constraint, `docs/DEVELOPMENT.md` section 2).
